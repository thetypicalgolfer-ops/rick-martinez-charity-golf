They Served.
Now It’s Our Turn.

📅 Thursday, October 1, 2026 ⛳ Cedar Creek Golf Course · San Antonio 🎖️ For our local homeless veterans

On a single night in America, 32,495 veterans have nowhere to sleep. One thousand eight hundred and thirty-seven of them are in Texas. Not in a war zone. Here. In the state they came home to.

Those are the federal Point-in-Time numbers — volunteers walking riverbanks and underpasses with clipboards on one cold January night, counting people. They are conservative by design. Everybody who works in this knows the real figure is higher, because a man sleeping in his truck behind a shopping centre does not get counted.

They wrote a blank check. We can at least pick up a tab.

🎖️ Who We Are Playing For

Veterans do not become homeless the way people imagine. It is rarely one dramatic collapse. It is a job that ends, a lease that will not renew, an injury that never quite healed, a marriage that came apart, a waiting list that is eleven months long. Somewhere in that chain a person who once had a rank and a role and a bunk ends up with a car and a gym membership for the shower.

They are disproportionately likely to be alone. They are disproportionately unlikely to ask. That combination is exactly why this problem needs other people to go looking for it.

📉 Here Is The Part That Should Give You Hope

Veteran homelessness in this country has fallen by 56% since 2009. More than half. In a single recent year the VA got 51,936 veterans into housing — the most in seven years.

That did not happen by itself and it did not happen because the problem got easier. It happened because communities decided it was unacceptable and then paid for the boring, unglamorous things that actually work: deposits, first month’s rent, a bus pass, a phone, a caseworker who picks up. This is one of the few social problems in America where showing up with money demonstrably moves the number.

It works. That is the whole reason to bother.

⛳ So Why A Golf Tournament?

Because it gets people in a room who would never come to a fundraiser. Because a foursome costs $600 and four friends split it without thinking twice. Because businesses that would ignore a donation letter will happily sponsor a hole and put their name on a sign.

And because for one morning a year, a few hundred people in San Antonio spend their day thinking about men and women they have never met. That is not nothing. That is how the 56% happened.

🇺🇸 What The Day Looks Like

Thursday 1 October at Cedar Creek Golf Course, 8250 Vista Colina. Check-in opens at 7:00 AM, shotgun start at 8:30. Four-person scramble, cart included, contests out on the course. $180 a player, $600 for a foursome.

Veterans, active-duty military and law enforcement — you are why we are out there. Thank you for standing with us.

But here’s the real reason we’re out here…

Behind every contest, every prize and every laugh on the course is somebody who once stood a post so the rest of us did not have to. Who signed a piece of paper agreeing, in writing, to give everything. Who came home to a country that said thank you for your service and then got busy.

Most of them are fine. Some of them are not. And the ones who are not will almost never tell you, because the same thing that made them good at the job — do not complain, do not be a burden, handle it — is exactly what keeps them from asking now.

They will not ask you for help.
That is precisely why somebody has to go and find them.
On October 1st, that is us.

Maybe golf isn’t your thing. Maybe a Thursday morning doesn’t work. Maybe you live a thousand miles from San Antonio. We understand — life is busy. But please hear this gently: a veteran sleeping in his car three miles from that first tee does not have a busy week coming up. He has a Thursday like every other Thursday.

So if you can play — play. If you can sponsor — sponsor. And if you truly can’t be there … then this is your swing.
